# Sounds

> Boundary: the sound set and the background music, the script that generates them, and how the
> game plays them through the audio engine. NOT covered: the audio engine's internals (its own documentation in `node_modules`).

## Purpose

A bowling alley is mostly sound: the rumble of the ball and the crash of the pins. Here every sound
comes from a committed script, so an open-source audience gets the source of every file.

## Rules

1. **Generation:** `npm run audio:build` runs `scripts/audio/generate.mjs`, a pure, seeded Node
   script that synthesises WAV files into `game/public/audio/`: `roll` (a seamless loop of
   low-passed noise and low rumble), `impact` (a dull thud), `pin-clack` (a short hard-wood click,
   several variants), `strike` (a short bright flourish). Parameters are named constants with a
   comment on the sound they shape. The WAV files are committed as build outputs of that script.
2. `npm run audio:check` regenerates into a temporary folder and fails if any committed file differs
   byte for byte (a gate in `check`).
3. **Playback** through the audio engine (`obsidian-eclipse-audio-engine`, Web Audio transport):
   `roll` follows the ball speed (gain and, if the engine supports it, playback rate; otherwise
   crossfaded speed bands — the choice and why go in «Rounds»); `impact` on each impact event;
   `pin-clack` on pin contacts above a contact-speed threshold, at most `MAX_CLACKS_PER_S`;
   `strike` on a score of ten.
4. **Muted until the first gesture**, as browsers require: a sound toggle in the HUD (localised,
   accessible); any click on the page also unlocks audio through the engine. Once unlocked, sound
   stays as the listener left it across runs — a new run never asks for another gesture — and the
   toggle alone decides on or off (the first click on the toggle turns sound on).
5. Values in `game/src/config/audio.ts`.
6. **Background music** (added by a human's request of 2026-10-27): one seamless loop,
   `music-alley`, generated by the same script from numbers: a cheerful, bouncy groove in a major
   key, ≈ 126 BPM — a walking bass, bright offbeat chord stabs, a catchy lead hook, handclaps and
   hi-hats (a human asked on 2026-10-27 for happier music than the first slow, warm groove at
   100 BPM), played through the audio
   engine's music controller (`music.play`, looping) from the first gesture, at `MUSIC_LEVEL`
   under the effects, ducked for the strike, silenced with the same sound toggle; whenever sound
   becomes audible again the music is playing.

## Acceptance criteria

1. [auto] `npm run audio:check` passes; `--selftest` rejects a modified WAV.
2. [auto] Unit test: the mapping from game events to sound cues (pure) gives the expected cues for
   a scripted run (one impact, eight pins, no strike).
3. [human] The rumble rises with speed and the pin crash sounds like wood, not glass.

## References

- Recordings to compare against (links only, nothing downloaded): bowling-alley ambience and
  strikes on Freesound, e.g. https://freesound.org/search/?q=bowling+strike and
  https://freesound.org/search/?q=bowling+alley+ambience.
- Synthesis of impacts: a struck solid as a sum of exponentially decaying modes plus a short noise
  burst (modal synthesis, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_synthesis); wood sounds short and
  «dull» because its modes decay fast, glass and metal ring because they do not (Cook, «Real
  Sound Synthesis for Interactive Applications», summarised at
  https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~prc/).
- RIFF/WAVE PCM header layout (http://soundfile.sapp.org/doc/WaveFormat/).
- The audio engine's README and types (`node_modules/obsidian-eclipse-audio-engine`): cues,
  held voices with volume and pan, unlock on a gesture.

## Rounds

- 2026-10-27 · round 1 · Built the seeded generator (`scripts/audio/`: WAV writer, filters,
  envelopes, the sound set), `npm run audio:build`, the `check-audio` gate (`npm run audio:check`,
  byte-for-byte against a fresh generation; its selftest flips one bit of `impact.wav`), the pure
  event → cue mapping, a player over the audio engine, the session's event stream (impacts, pin
  contacts from Havok collision events, the result) and a sound toggle. Playback rate: the engine's
  held voices take volume and pan but no rate, so the rumble is **two crossfaded speed bands** —
  `roll-slow` (180 Hz noise, 38 Hz rumble) and `roll-fast` (420 Hz noise, 55 Hz rumble), an
  equal-power crossfade from 6 to 12 m/s. Pin clacks: four variants of three modes between 700
  and 2 480 Hz decaying in 25 ms plus a 3 ms click — short and low enough to read as wood; the
  strike is a C-major arpeggio with a bell decay. Files: 8 WAVs, 22 050 Hz mono, 285 KB. Wrong on
  the way, found in the browser: the player held the rumble before the files were decoded (an
  error every frame), and decoding never started — the engine decodes only once its audio context
  runs, after the first gesture → the player warms on the first unlock and plays nothing before.
  Then: one click turns the toggle to «Sound on», the 8 files load (200), a run to the result logs
  no error. Unit tests: the scripted run (one impact, eight pins) gives one thud, six clacks, no
  strike; ten gives the strike; clacks capped at 14 per second; the crossfade ends. Whether it
  sounds like wood and rumble is criterion 3, for a human with speakers.

- 2026-10-27 · round 2 · Rule 6, music, asked for by the human. `scripts/audio/music.mjs` writes a
  four-bar groove at 100 BPM (I–vi–IV–V in C: plucked bass on beats 1 and 3, a soft detuned pad,
  kick on 1 and 3, a brushed snare on 2 and 4, eighth-note hats), every note added with
  wrap-around so the 9.6 s loop has no seam; 423 KB. `normalise` used `Math.max(...samples)`,
  which overflows the call stack on a buffer this long → a loop; the earlier sounds stay byte for
  byte the same (`check-audio`). The player starts it with the engine's music controller at the
  first gesture, at `MUSIC_LEVEL` 0.35, and ducks it for the strike; the toggle mutes it with the
  rest. In the browser the 9 files load (200) and a run to the result logs no error. How it sounds
  is criterion 3, for a human with speakers; a criterion for the music is proposed in
  [review](../review.md).

- 2026-10-27 · round 3 · Reported by the human: no music, and a new run lost the effects. Causes,
  all in this repository's code: (a) the engine starts music only if sound is audible when the
  file is ready, and on Web Audio it does not restart it on unmute — while the HUD let the page's
  «first press» listener unlock and the toggle's click mute again in the same gesture, and the
  engine's default mute preference survived reloads in `localStorage`; (b) «Run again» reloaded the
  page, which closes the audio context, and the browser wants a new gesture. Rule 4 now says sound
  survives runs; rule 6 that the music plays whenever sound is audible. Built: the player keeps
  mute in memory (`inMemoryMutePreference`), turns sound on at the first press anywhere, leaves the
  toggle's own presses to the toggle, and (re)starts the music each time sound becomes audible;
  «Run again» stays in the page ([HUD](../game/hud.md)). Unit tests with a fake engine (first
  press, first toggle, unmute restarts music, a page press does not undo the toggle, muted cues
  silent). In the browser, read through `window.__bowling.soundStatus()`: running with
  `music-alley` after the first press, still after «Run again» in the same page, muted and back
  with the toggle; 9 files, no errors.
- 2026-10-27 · round 4 · Happier music (rule 6 rewritten): eight bars in C major at 126 BPM — a
  walking bass, offbeat chord stabs with a soft square tone, a lead hook with vibrato, kick on every
  beat, handclaps on 2 and 4, hi-hats on the eighths. Measured on the WAV: 15.24 s, peak 0.75,
  tempo from the onset autocorrelation 126.0 BPM, the jump at the loop point (0.0076) below the
  signal's own sample-to-sample step (0.019). `audio:check` passes. Not judged: how it sounds —
  that is criterion 3's human, with speakers.
- 2026-10-28 · round 5 · The rumble's speed bands now crossfade from `MIN_ROLL_SPEED_MPS` (3 m/s)
  to `RELEASE_SPEED_MPS` (10 m/s), the slowest and fastest a ball goes since the run became a throw
  that slows down. No sound file changed.

## Canonical files

- `scripts/audio/sounds.mjs` holds every sound's parameters; it imports the domain's seeded
  generator, so the scripts run under tsx.
- `game/src/audio/cues.ts` is pure (`cuesFor`, `rollMix`); `sound-player.ts` adapts the engine;
  `connect-sound.ts` plays a session (store → rumble, events → cues); `ui/use-sound.ts` owns the
  player for the page and unlocks it on any pointer press.
